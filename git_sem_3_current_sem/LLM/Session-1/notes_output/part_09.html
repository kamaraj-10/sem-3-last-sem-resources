Unknown words in a real application: what happens, and how big vocabularies help

[2:08:41 – 2:10:03]
The concept

Student: "In the real world a rare word may not be in the vocabulary because of the min-frequency hyperparameter. How do we include it?" Answer: it becomes the unknown token.

  • The unknown token ([UNK]) is represented differently in each model. "Maybe OOV is one token, unknown is one token."
  • If unknown tokens appear often, "you won't get the correct output". "That will be a problem."
  • Frontier models avoid this with a very large vocabulary. That is "the reason they are massive models" (partly, the embedding table is large too). They also use byte-level fallback (part 05).
  • Exercise: search the 2026 models and find their published vocabulary sizes.
Intuition + layman analogy

A translator with a small dictionary writes "[unknown word]" whenever a word is missing. If that happens in every sentence, the translation is nonsense. A bigger dictionary, or the ability to spell words out letter by letter, fixes it.

Scenario illustrative numbers

How often does [UNK] hurt? A 40-token sentence

Tokens per sentence40
Word-level vocabulary, [UNK] rate per token3 %
Byte-level BPE, [UNK] rate per token0 %
Step-by-step computation
1
Expected [UNK] tokens per sentence, word-level. 40 × 0.03 = 1.2
2
Probability a sentence has no [UNK] (independent tokens). 0.9740 = e40 ln 0.97 = e−1.218 ≈ 0.296
3
So the share of sentences with at least one [UNK]. 1 − 0.296 = 0.704 ≈ 70 %
4
Byte-level BPE. 0 % per token → 0 % of sentences
≈ 70 % of sentences hit [UNK]a small per-token rate becomes a large per-sentence rate
Justification
  • A large vocabulary captures more words as single tokens, so fewer [UNK] and fewer tokens per sentence.
  • "Nowadays we don't see that gibberish, otherwise they would start hallucinating."
  • A bigger vocabulary enlarges the embedding table and the output softmax layer (|V| × d each; part 06).
coverage and shorter sequences vs larger embedding and output layers.
Common mistakes
  • Assuming a rare word is silently handled. With a non-byte tokenizer it becomes [UNK] and information is lost.
  • Thinking min_frequency changes the base alphabet. It only controls which merges are made.

🧠 Quick recall

1. What is produced when a word is not in a non-byte tokenizer's vocabulary, and what is the consequence?
An unknown token ([UNK] / OOV). The information about the word is lost, so output quality can drop ("you won't get correct output").
2. Why do frontier models have such big vocabularies?
Fewer unknown tokens, shorter sequences and better coverage of words and languages. The cost is a bigger embedding and output matrix.
3. A tokenizer gives [UNK] for 2 % of tokens. What fraction of 50-token sentences has no [UNK]?
0.9850 = e50 × (−0.0202) = e−1.01 ≈ 0.364. So about 64 % of sentences contain at least one [UNK].
4. Name the three textbooks recommended and why not hard copies.
Hands-On Large Language Models (Alammar), Speech and Language Processing (Jurafsky and Martin, online), Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch) (Raschka). The field changes fast and online versions update often.

What is on Takshila (the lecture slides and handout).
